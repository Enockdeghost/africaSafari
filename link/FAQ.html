Tanzania Travel FAQs

What is the best time to visit Tanzania?

The best time to visit Tanzania depends on your interests:

  • June-October: Dry season - best for wildlife viewing and climbing Kilimanjaro
  • January-February: Calving season in Serengeti - great for predator action
  • November-December: Short rains - fewer tourists, lush landscapes
  • March-May: Long rainy season - some parks may be inaccessible

For beach holidays, Zanzibar is great year-round except during the peak rains in April/May.

Do I need a visa to enter Tanzania?

Most visitors require a visa to enter Tanzania. Here's what you need to know:

  • Visas can be obtained on arrival at major entry points or online via the e-visa system
  • Cost is approximately $50 USD for most nationalities (single entry)
  • Your passport must be valid for at least 6 months from your date of entry
  • Proof of yellow fever vaccination may be required if traveling from an endemic area

Always check current requirements with the Tanzanian embassy in your country before traveling.

What vaccinations are required for Tanzania?

Recommended vaccinations include:

  • Yellow Fever: Required if arriving from an endemic area
  • Hepatitis A & B: Recommended for all travelers
  • Typhoid: Recommended, especially if visiting rural areas
  • Rabies: Recommended for long stays or adventure travelers
  • Malaria Prophylaxis: Essential for all visitors

Consult a travel health specialist 6-8 weeks before your trip for personalized advice.

Is Tanzania safe for tourists?

Tanzania is generally safe for tourists, but you should take standard precautions:

  • Violent crime against tourists is rare, but petty theft occurs in cities
  • Use registered taxis and avoid walking alone at night in urban areas
  • National parks are very safe when following guide instructions
  • Zanzibar is generally safe, but respect local conservative dress codes
  • Be cautious of scams in tourist areas

Register with your embassy and purchase comprehensive travel insurance.

What currency is used in Tanzania?

The official currency is the Tanzanian Shilling (TZS). Key information:

  • US Dollars are widely accepted, especially for tours and larger purchases
  • Bring newer US dollar bills (post-2006) as older bills may not be accepted
  • Credit cards are accepted in larger hotels and businesses in cities
  • ATMs are available in major towns and cities
  • Always carry small denominations for tips and small purchases
What languages are spoken in Tanzania?

Tanzania is linguistically diverse:

  • Swahili: The national language spoken by nearly all citizens
  • English: Official language used in government, business, and tourism
  • Local languages: Over 120 tribal languages are spoken throughout the country

In tourist areas, you'll find most people speak English. Learning a few Swahili phrases will enhance your experience:

  • Jambo! - Hello!
  • Asante sana - Thank you very much
  • Habari gani? - How are you?
  • Karibu - Welcome
What are the must-visit places in Tanzania?

Tanzania offers incredible diversity:

  • Serengeti National Park: World-famous for the Great Migration
  • Mount Kilimanjaro: Africa's highest peak
  • Ngorongoro Crater: Unique volcanic caldera teeming with wildlife
  • Zanzibar: Historic Stone Town and beautiful beaches
  • Selous Game Reserve: Africa's largest game reserve
  • Tarangire National Park: Famous for large elephant herds
  • Lake Manyara: Birdwatcher's paradise with tree-climbing lions

Consider spending at least 10-14 days to experience multiple destinations.

Can I climb Mount Kilimanjaro? Do I need a guide?

Yes, you can climb Kilimanjaro with proper preparation:

  • Guides are mandatory for all climbers
  • Choose from 7 established routes with varying difficulty
  • The climb takes 5-9 days depending on route and acclimatization
  • Best months are January-February and July-October
  • Physical fitness is important, but technical climbing skills are not required

Always book with a reputable operator that follows ethical practices for porters.

What wildlife can I see in the Serengeti?

The Serengeti is home to an incredible array of wildlife:

  • Big Five: Lion, leopard, elephant, buffalo, and rhino (though rhinos are rare)
  • Great Migration: Over 1.5 million wildebeest and 200,000 zebras
  • Predators: Cheetahs, hyenas, jackals
  • Herbivores: Giraffes, hippos, antelopes, gazelles
  • Birds: Over 500 species including ostriches and vultures

The best wildlife viewing is during the dry season (June-October) and during calving season (January-February).

What is the Great Migration?

The Great Migration is one of nature's most spectacular events:

  • Annual movement of over 1.5 million wildebeest and hundreds of thousands of zebras and gazelles
  • Continuous clockwise circuit through Serengeti (Tanzania) and Masai Mara (Kenya)
  • Driven by the search for fresh grazing and water
  • Highlights include river crossings (July-October) and calving season (January-February)
  • Predators follow the herds, creating dramatic wildlife interactions

This is considered one of the "Seven New Wonders of the World".

What should I pack for a safari in Tanzania?

Essential safari packing list:

  • Clothing: Neutral colors (khaki, green, brown), light layers, warm jacket for evenings
  • Footwear: Comfortable walking shoes and sandals
  • Accessories: Wide-brimmed hat, sunglasses, binoculars, camera with zoom lens
  • Health: Sunscreen (high SPF), insect repellent, personal medications
  • Documents: Passport, visa, vaccination certificate, travel insurance
  • Extras: Power bank, headlamp, reusable water bottle

Avoid blue and black clothing which attracts tsetse flies.

Are there any cultural norms I should be aware of?

Respecting local customs enhances your experience:

  • Greetings: Always greet people before starting conversations
  • Dress: Modest dress is appreciated, especially in Zanzibar and rural areas
  • Photography: Always ask permission before photographing people
  • Public Affection: Avoid kissing and hugging in public
  • Left Hand: Considered unclean - use right hand for giving/receiving
  • Religion: Respect Islamic customs in Zanzibar during Ramadan

Learning a few Swahili phrases will be greatly appreciated by locals.

What is the food like in Tanzania?

Tanzanian cuisine is diverse and flavorful:

  • Staples: Ugali (maize porridge), rice, chapati, plantains
  • Popular Dishes: Nyama choma (grilled meat), pilau rice, samosas, chipsi mayai (chips omelette)
  • Coastal Specialties: Seafood curry, biryani, Zanzibar pizza
  • Fruits: Mangoes, pineapples, passion fruit, jackfruit
  • Drinks: Chai (spiced tea), fresh juices, local beers

Vegetarians will find good options, especially at Indian restaurants which are common.

How is the internet and mobile connectivity in Tanzania?

Connectivity varies across the country:

  • Major cities and towns have good 4G coverage
  • National parks and remote areas have limited or no connectivity
  • SIM cards are inexpensive and available at airports (Vodacom, Airtel, Tigo)
  • WiFi is available in most hotels and restaurants in urban areas
  • Power outages can affect connectivity, especially in rural areas

Consider disconnecting during safari for a more immersive experience.

Can I drink tap water in Tanzania?

It's not recommended to drink tap water in Tanzania:

  • Stick to bottled or filtered water throughout your stay
  • Ensure bottled water seals are intact
  • Use bottled water for brushing teeth
  • Avoid ice in drinks unless you're at a high-end establishment
  • Peel fruits and avoid raw vegetables washed in tap water

Carry a reusable water bottle with a filter for eco-friendly hydration.

What type of electrical plugs are used in Tanzania?

Tanzania uses Type D and Type G electrical sockets:

  • Type D: Three round pins in a triangular pattern
  • Type G: Three rectangular pins in a triangular pattern (British standard)
  • Voltage is 230V with a frequency of 50Hz

Bring a universal adapter that covers both types. Power cuts are common, so a power bank is useful.

What are the options for accommodation in Tanzania?

Accommodation ranges from basic to ultra-luxurious:

  • Luxury Lodges: High-end safari camps with premium amenities
  • Tented Camps: Comfortable tents with en-suite bathrooms
  • Mid-range Hotels: Comfortable lodging in towns and cities
  • Budget Options: Hostels and guesthouses
  • Beach Resorts: Range from boutique to all-inclusive in Zanzibar

Book well in advance during peak season (July-October and December-January).

How can I get around in Tanzania?

Transportation options include:

  • Domestic Flights: Efficient between major parks and Zanzibar
  • Safari Vehicles: 4x4 Land Cruisers with pop-up roofs for game viewing
  • Buses: Reliable coach services between major cities
  • Taxis: Available in urban areas - agree on fare before departure
  • Dala-dala: Local minibuses - cheap but crowded
  • Ferries: Connect mainland with Zanzibar and other islands

For safaris, travel with licensed operators in properly equipped vehicles.

What are the shopping opportunities in Tanzania?

Popular souvenirs and shopping experiences:

  • Tingatinga Paintings: Colorful Tanzanian folk art
  • Makonde Carvings: Intricate wood carvings
  • Kangas & Kitenges: Traditional colorful fabrics
  • Spices: Zanzibar is famous for cloves, vanilla, cinnamon
  • Tanzanite: Rare gemstone found only in Tanzania
  • Local Markets: Experience vibrant market culture

Always bargain at markets but do so respectfully. Buy gemstones only from reputable dealers.

What are the emergency contact numbers in Tanzania?

Important contacts for emergencies:

  • General Emergency: 112 (from mobile phones)
  • Police: 111
  • Fire Department: 114
  • Medical Emergency: 115
  • Tourist Police: +255 22 211 0754 (Dar es Salaam)

Also save contact information for:

  • Your country's embassy or consulate
  • Your tour operator or hotel
  • Your travel insurance emergency assistance line
